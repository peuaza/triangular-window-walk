# AUDIT_V2 — every numeric claim in `bthm_jalc_v2.tex`, sections 1–6

*Scope: main text lines 25–872 (abstract, §1–§6). The appendix's tables (`_appendix_evidence.tex`)
are not audited here, but the two body claims whose only source is an appendix table
(step counts 0.017/15.8 and the exponent 1.50–2.60) are, because they also stand in the abstract.*

*Rule applied: a number is VERIFIED only if it was regenerated from a JSON/script and matched at the
precision printed. A report's prose is never sufficient on its own; where a claim exists only as
report prose it is marked `report-only` in the source column and the verdict says so. A claim with
no traceable producer is NO SOURCE.*

## Headline

79 numeric claims audited: **39 VERIFIED** (regenerated from a JSON/script and equal at the printed
precision), **30 MISMATCH**, **3 NO SOURCE** in the table (a further four numbers or sub-numbers have
no producer at all — §G), **6 MACHINE-UNLABELLED sites** (tables and paragraphs that print a number
without the device that made it) and **7 NARRATIVE**. The four that matter most:

- the abstract's step-count range ("0.017 to 15.8 … a factor of nine hundred") has no producer and its
  low end is wrong by ~400× (canonical measures 7.04 in the same units);

- longrun's mean law quotes the asymptotic form as the measured fit;
- Table `tab:threecosts` prints 66 numbers with no machine and no charge rule, against the paper's own
  promise that every number carries the machine that made it.

## E. MISMATCH, in priority order (with the sentence to change)

Priority = how far the printed number is from a regenerated one, weighted by whether the claim
carries an argument.

1. **A1/A2 — the "no clock" paragraph, and the abstract.** The low end of the step-count range does
   not exist in the data. Regenerated: canonical 7.04, NBLK>3 0.385, so the span is 18× (41× if
   hashmod's 15.8 is right), not 900×; the exponent range is 1.70–2.20, not 1.50–2.60.
   *Abstract:* "at n=4096 the measured step count ranges from 0.017 to 15.8 times n^{2}/128, a factor
   of nine hundred, and the growth exponent between n=1024 and 4096 ranges from 1.50 to 2.60."
   *§1:* "the mean step count ranges from 0.017 to 15.8 times n^{2}/128 across the rules, a factor of
   nine hundred" and "the growth exponent between n=1024 and 4096 ranges from 1.50 to 2.60".
   *§2.1:* same numbers. Change to the regenerated values, or re-run the step-count grid at n=4096 for
   all 21 rules and store it as one JSON, since the appendix table has no producer.

2. **A21 — the epoch-failure fraction.** "0.3–36 per cent" is wrong by up to 2× at the top and 5× at
   the bottom. Regenerated from the per-tape `nep`/`bad` fields: 0.06–17.1 % over the five named
   rules. *Sentence:* "the epoch law fails in mid-tape on a 0.3–36 per cent set of epochs
   (dense2, trip, oddrun, canonical, MAXP)". Change to "0.3–17 per cent".

3. **D1–D4 — the kappa table is one pass stale.** Every value in the printed table matches
   `probe_kappa_inc`, not `probe_kappa_inc2`, which is the same measurement after the checkpoint fix
   and with `nt` 6000/4000 instead of 4000/3000. The two biggest moves: trip at 4096 0.510 → 0.472,
   peak at 4096 0.585 → 0.614. The trends survive; the numbers do not.
   *Caption + table:* replace the five rows with the `ki2_five.json` c16 values, or state that the
   table is the pre-fix pass (which the paper says it is not).

4. **A15 — longrun's mean cost law.** "its mean is 4.87+0.45 ln n reads per cell" quotes the

## F. MACHINE-UNLABELLED

The rule the paper promises: every number carries the machine and the charge rule that produced it.
These do not.

**M1 — Table `tab:threecosts` (11 rows × 6 columns) names no machine and no charge.** The cells come
from `probe_k300/k3_*.json`, where each file states `machine = "hmach3.H3 B=64"` and
`charge = "band+frontier: 1 per gets/getsg/raw touch, + opening pass"`. The caption says only "G and e
are measured on the same machine", which identifies the *pairing*, not the device. Add a `machine`
column (or a caption sentence naming `hmach3.H3 B=64` and the charge) — otherwise every one of the 66
numbers is unlabelled, and the four values in a row can be moved a factor 2–3 by changing the device.

**M2 — Table 3 (level-count rules) has a `machine` column but no charge rule.** "band + frontier,
B=64" is half the identity; the charge (`1` per `gets`/`getsg`/`raw` touch, plus the opening pass) is
what turns a touch count into "reads per test". Also the two NBLK rows are recorded in the producing
report as `band+frontier+certifier` (`HYBRID3.md` §2, lines 16–17), so the machine column understates
the device for those rows.

**M3 — the kappa table names the charge ("incremental") but not the device.** "ridge regression on 41
bits" is in the prose, 20 lines below; the predictor's budget grid (`c ∈ {1,…,128}`) and the register
count are in the appendix. A reader who takes the table alone cannot tell whether 0.799 is a ridge, a
CES, or a covering code — and the paper's own §6 says the device changes κ by a power.

**M4 — §3's "147.7 n ... 116.5 n" is exemplary; §5's `1.12 n²` is not.** The canonical numbers carry
machine names in three places. The 1.12 n² constant does not: it is the `plateau` tape family, not a
bound, and the tape family is the machine here as much as the device is.

**M5 — the abstract's "reads per test flat (3.4–5.3)" and "cost at most 1.12 n²"** carry no device,
while the body's Table 3 does name `band + frontier, B=64`. Since the abstract is what a referee
quotes, at least "band+frontier machine" should appear beside the constant.

**M6 — the ladder table names mechanisms, not devices.** "Brent cycle detection", "A = s/31 anchors",
"a witness tape", "one window read per test" identify the *idea* of each rung, not a machine that was
run. That is acceptable for a ladder of mechanisms, but it means no rung's number is reproducible from
the table; the caption should point at the probe directory that built each machine, as Table 3's
caption does not either.

## H. The four checks the brief asked for, explicitly

**(1) A grid exponent must be quoted with its fitting window and K.** Table `tab:threecosts`'s caption
does state the window ("log–log exponents over n = 512…4096"), and its g column states K implicitly
(the nine-tape panel). The two exponents that fail this test are in §3's prose: "g = n^{2.00–2.02} for
fourteen of nineteen rules, against n^{−0.36–2.17} for the sample maxima" gives neither the window nor
that the two ranges come from *different estimators* on the same sizes, and "the measured a is 0.08 to
0.98 depending on how the tail is defined" gives no window and no definition (the two definitions in
`tail_f.py` give 0.08–0.98 and 0.25–1.57 respectively). Both sentences need "over n = 512…4096, from
`gw_fit.json`" / "from `tail_f.py`, cut at 10× the median".

**(2) A mean and a maximum must come from the same machine.** The paper gets this right where it
matters most — canonical 116.5 vs 147.7 is called out, and Table `tab:threecosts` says G and e share a
machine — but the g column is a *different estimator* (witness panel) from G and e (per-tape sample),
so `g/e` in that table is a cross-estimator ratio. That is intentional and stated ("g_ρ is the maximum
over the nine-tape witness panel"), but the "No row has matching exponents: all eleven are non-perfect"
claim then rests on comparing a panel exponent with a sample exponent. For AMAX, e's exponent is 2.13
(sample) and g's is 2.02 (panel); on the sample maxima g's exponent is **2.111** — the perfect-rule
verdict for AMAX/AMIN does not change, but for BAL>m/2 the sample-max exponent is −0.36 and the panel
one 2.00, so the row's non-perfection is an artefact of the estimator pair. Recommend printing both g
exponents (panel and sample) in the table, as §3 already does in prose.

**(3) g must come from a witness.** Done for the nine rules with a panel entry; the two NBLK rows are
blank ("—"), correctly. The residual defect is §3's "eight of nineteen" count (A18) and the 0.017
endpoint (A1). The BAL>m/2 negative sample exponent (−0.36) is exactly the failure the brief warns
about and the paper does quote it, so this check passes where it is made explicit.

**(4) Every table row must name its machine and its charge rule.** Table `tab:threecosts` fails both
(M1); Table 3 fails the charge (M2); the kappa table fails the device (M3); the ladder table names
mechanisms rather than devices ("Brent cycle detection", "A = s/31 anchors") which is acceptable for a
ladder but means the rungs are not reproducible from the table alone (M6, new).
