A memoryless two-header tape machine: what a window costs, and why two lower-bound techniques fail

## Description

A tape of $n$ cells over three values — equivalently, two-bit cells read through their sum — is read
by two headers that enclose a window; at each step a fixed rule, given the window's content and the
geometry, chooses one of eight actions: move a header inward or outward, trim a cell from the left,
the right or both, or halt. The cost is the number of cell reads, the state is $O(\log n)$ bits, and
the question is how the cost must grow with $n$ for each rule. On the evidence the rules fall into
three classes — fully linear, semi-linear and superlinear — separated by a worst-case cost and a mean
cost. All three are populated: four natural rules are fully linear, one construction is semi-linear
($e=\Theta(n)$ with $g=\Theta(n^2)$), and the remaining seventeen are superlinear.

The paper separates the length of a walk from the number of cells it reads, shows the length is a
property of the rule rather than of the model, gives machines for every rule in the catalogue, arranges
their costs on a ladder from $n^{1/2}$ to $n^3$, and states exactly which rungs are proved, which are
conditional and which are conjectural. Negative results included: the certificate bound does not
compose (on one tape per-query certificates sum to $1{,}285{,}703$ cells while their union has
$1{,}020$), and a bound on the rule's output transfers to the walk only after multiplication by the
fraction of queries at which the answer can change the action, measured at $0.104$ and falling like
$n^{-0.99}$.
