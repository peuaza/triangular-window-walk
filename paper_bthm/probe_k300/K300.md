# K = 300 meeting tapes: do the band family's sub-quadratic e exponents survive?

## 0. Answer

**The sub-quadratic exponents survive K = 300 -- they do not move to 2.0.** On the four sizes 512..4096, at 300 meeting tapes wherever the caps allowed it:

| rule | quoted (256..4096, K 60-200) | base, 512..4096, its own K | **K=300** | 95% band from sd(e)/sqrt(K) | change vs base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `KCHG` | 1.29 | 1.38 | **1.49** | 1.28-1.69 | +0.11 |
| `BAL>m/2` | 1.57 | 1.66 | **1.62** | 1.42-1.83 | -0.04 |
| `BAL>m/4` | 1.61 | 1.56 | **1.56** | 1.37-1.75 | +0.00 |
| `NBLK=1` | - | 1.68 | **1.81** | 1.61-2.00 | +0.13 |
| `NBLK>2` | - | 1.83 | **1.79** | 1.63-1.95 | -0.04 |
| `NBLK>3` | 1.69 | 1.73 | **1.83** | 1.66-2.00 | +0.10 |
| `AMAX` | - | 2.13 | **2.13** | 1.93-2.34 | +0.01 |
| `AMIN` | - | 1.79 | **1.85** | 1.66-2.04 | +0.06 |
| `MAXP` | - | 1.88 | **1.89** | 1.78-2.00 | +0.01 |
| `LAS` | - | 1.96 | **1.97** | 1.82-2.11 | +0.01 |
| `oddrun` | - | 2.13 | **2.15** | 2.04-2.26 | +0.02 |

No rule moves by more than 0.13, every shift sits inside the band on either exponent, and **no rule that was sub-quadratic in the base becomes quadratic at K = 300**. What the old probe read at K = 60-200 is what the same machine on the same tapes reads at K = 300: **the small-K artefact is not the explanation.**

(Two windows are in play and they are not interchangeable. The *quoted* column is the paper's figure for the five-point fit 256..4096; the *base* column is the same probe restricted to 512..4096 so that it is comparable with the K=300 fit. A plain five-point OLS on `MASTER_FINAL`'s own cells gives 1.33 / 1.53 / 1.59 / 1.65, i.e. the quoted values are 0.03-0.04 steeper than that, so they were fitted on a slightly different window or weighting -- worth resolving before the number is printed again. Either way the five-point value is the lower one, because the n=256 point sits **above** the 512..4096 line for `KCHG` (+24%), `BAL>m/2` (+49%) and `NBLK>3` (+31%). All comparisons below use the four-point window on both sides.)

Two things do change the claim, and both are in the data rather than in the sampling:

1. **`NBLK>3` leaves the 1.2-1.7 cluster** (quoted 1.69, base 1.73, K=300 **1.83**); `NBLK=1` and `NBLK>2` sit at 1.81 and 1.79. The cluster is `KCHG` 1.49, `BAL>m/2` 1.62, `BAL>m/4` 1.56 -- three rules, not six.
2. **The sub-quadratic number is a property of the mean, not of the rule.** The median meeting cost is *exactly* linear: `p50/n` = 5.3 for `KCHG`, 5.3 for `BAL>m/2`, 5.4 for `BAL>m/4` at every size (p50 exponent 0.99-1.02). The mean sits above that floor by a factor that grows from 2.2-2.8 at n=512 to 7.0-9.2 at n=4096, and that growth is the whole of the sub-quadratic exponent. Meanwhile `g_K` grows like `n^2` for every band rule (2.06, 2.07, 1.91): the tail the mean integrates over *is* quadratic. `e` lies between two orders because it is a mixture of them.

So the sentence the paper can defend is not "the band family has sub-quadratic mean cost" but "**the band family's mean cost has exponent 1.5-1.8 on n <= 4096, between a mandatory Theta(n) pass and a Theta(n^2) tail whose weight grows with n**". The exponent is a crossover value that drifts up with n by construction; it is not an asymptotic order, and 1.49 at n = 4096 is not where it stops.

## 3. Is K = 300 enough? e(K) on the same tape set

Each cell keeps its meeting reads in **draw order**, so `e(K)` for any `K <= nmeet` is the mean of the first K -- byte for byte what a probe with target K would have recorded. The K=100 -> K=max change is therefore a *paired* measurement on one tape set, and it is the sensitive test of the artefact hypothesis. **Direction:** a small K understates a heavy-tailed mean, so if small K is what flattened the old exponents the exponent must **rise** as K grows, and the rise must be larger at the top size than at the bottom. A flat or falling column refutes it.

The paired change from K=100 to the largest K the cell allows is small and **does not go the way the artefact needs**. The biggest is `KCHG` +0.24 (to K=300); then `NBLK>3` +0.11 and `NBLK=1` +0.08, while the two `BAL` rules move **down** (-0.10 and -0.11) and the rest are +-0.06 or unresolvable (K stops at 100). Against a band of about +-0.2, and with both signs present, this is not a systematic small-K flattening. Only `KCHG` climbs monotonically (1.19 at K=50 to 1.49 at K=300), and even there the top point is still moving (e@4096: 87k at K=100, 128k at K=200, 151k at K=300) -- a statement about how heavy the tail is at n=4096, not about the machinery of the old probe.

Read the other way: **`e` is not converged at K=300, but neither was it converged at the K the old probe used -- both are samples of a mean with CV 1.5-2.8.** Fixing K at 300 does not remove the noise; it makes the noise the *same shape* at all four sizes, which is what the exponent needs.

For `KCHG`, `BAL>m/2` and `BAL>m/4` the median is the same multiple of n at every size -- 5.3, 5.3, 5.4 -- i.e. a **Theta(n) floor** (the opening pass every run pays), and the mean is 2.0-2.8x that floor at 512 and 7.0-9.2x at 4096. The p90/p95 exponents are already 1.4-2.1. So the mean's exponent is set by how much of the mass has climbed out of the floor into the tail, and that share grows with n.

This is why `e < g` with `g ~ n^2` is not a contradiction: `g_K` is a maximum, which lives in the tail, and the tail is quadratic; `e` is the mixture average and sits between the floor and the tail.

**Identity check.** In 17 of 27 cells whose old probe held at least as many meetings, the K=300 maximum equals the old cell's maximum to the digit. In the one cell where the old probe held **exactly** 300 meetings -- the only place the two samples coincide, `BAL>m/2`@1024 -- the entire 300-element read vector is identical, not merely its mean (16834.280 both). The two probes are the same machine on the same tapes, not a re-implementation.

## 5. Verdict per rule

- `KCHG`: **e exponent 1.49 at K=300, so the sub-quadratic cluster is real** -- band 1.28-1.69, base 1.38, shift +0.24 (K 100->300). The median is linear (p50 exp 0.99, p50/n = 5.3), so this is a mean-over-tail exponent, not an order.
- `BAL>m/2`: **e exponent 1.62 at K=300, so the sub-quadratic cluster is real** -- band 1.42-1.83, base 1.66, shift -0.10 (K 100->250) [4096 K=279]. The median is linear (p50 exp 0.99, p50/n = 5.3), so this is a mean-over-tail exponent, not an order.
- `BAL>m/4`: **e exponent 1.56 at K=300, so the sub-quadratic cluster is real** -- band 1.37-1.75, base 1.56, shift -0.11 (K 100->250) [4096 K=282]. The median is linear (p50 exp 1.02, p50/n = 5.4), so this is a mean-over-tail exponent, not an order.
